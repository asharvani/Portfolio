Unlocking the ‘Aha!’ moment: boosting engagement and retention in SwiftChat.

In short

The problem

Despite thousands of installs each month, many users dropped off after onboarding without engaging further. This directly impacted retention.

What I designed

A contextual guided conversation in place of the old app tour, shown as a popup the first time someone lands on Discover.

What changed

D7 retention up 33%, D15 up 66% and M1 up 25% against a control group that never saw the message.

The problem: users were leaving too soon

SwiftChat is an AI-powered edtech platform. We envisioned an interactive space where students could learn through AI-driven conversations, quizzes, and challenges.

But something was off. Users were leaving too soon. So we took a deep dive into our user data and found some alarming patterns.

A majority of users abandoned the app within their first 72 hours, leading to a sharp decline in retention and engagement.

Why are users quitting so quickly? And how do we make their first experience truly engaging?

Understanding the user

To validate our assumptions, we spoke to students in classrooms and at home to understand their frustrations.

What users told us

“I installed the app but didn’t know what to do next.”

“I looked around a bit, but nothing felt interesting.”

“I didn’t see a reason to start chatting with the bot right away.”

01 · Too many options

Users were unsure what to do after onboarding, as soon as they landed on the Discover page.

02 · Low awareness of features

Many users didn’t explore quizzes and challenges because they weren’t aware of the variety of features.

03 · No urgency to interact

Users did not feel a strong motivation to start conversations with the bot.

Finding the root cause

Before UX, I was a dentist. One habit stayed with me: don’t treat the symptom. So we looked for the single behaviour that separated users who stayed from users who left.

That set the brief. Not a better tour of every feature. A guided nudge towards one first conversation, in a way that felt natural and exciting.

  1. What we saw

    24.16% of new users quit within three days.

  2. Why?

    They landed on Discover, saw too many options, and nothing told them where to start.

  3. What the data showed

    Users who had a bot conversation within the first 3 days were 2.5 times more likely to stay until the end of the month.

  4. Hypothesis

    Users will be more engaged if they are introduced to fun, interactive elements as soon as they onboard.

How might we get new users talking to a bot right after onboarding?

Key screens

From a passive tour to one well-timed conversation. Tested against a control group that didn’t see the message.

Before · App tour

A tour points at things. It doesn’t give a reason.

The previous onboarding walked users through the Bot Store in four steps: bots you might like, finding bots, filters and sorting. Useful, but it asked nothing of them.

four screens, zero reasons to chat
After · Guided popup

Replace the tour with a first conversation.

We replaced the old app tour with a contextual guided conversation, sent as an interstitial popup when users visited the Discover page for the first time.

One clear action: start a quiz with a bot. We did not send it to all users, keeping a control group for comparison.

guide them, don’t make them explore
Tested variants

Same popup, two reasons to tap.

We tested 2 variations of the design: one with fact-based messaging and one with FOMO-based messaging.

FOMO won

what won

FOMO-based messaging was highly effective in driving engagement. Timely nudges helped users take action immediately instead of passively exploring.

A small change, a big impact

What went well, and the one thing I’d carry into any onboarding.

Nudges beat tours

Replacing a passive onboarding with a guided conversation increased retention and encouraged meaningful interactions.

Test, then trust

A data-driven approach with a control group let us measure success and iterate quickly.

Small, strategic changes

This experiment proved that small, well-placed nudges can have a big impact on retention.

What I learned

It’s not just about getting users to sign up. It’s about making them stay, and that starts with engaging them from day one.
